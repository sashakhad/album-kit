## p1 — Balearic terrace

Balearic house in E major, built on a nylon-string guitar figure picked close to the bridge and a Juno pad sitting behind it like haze. 120 BPM, kick four to the floor and soft-edged, hats loose and a shade behind the grid, congas played by hand. Open on the guitar alone through tape delay and slide the kick under it at bar seventeen, then make the first release the pad widening across the stereo field until the room sounds twice as wide. Breakdown is the guitar and one long chord with the reverb tail left exposed, and the last third adds a four-note piano answer and leaves the filter open all the way. A tiled terrace at golden hour, chairs still wet from the hose, everyone facing the same direction. Late-80s Ibiza terrace patience, Balearic dub attitude.

## p2 — Piano house lift

Piano house in G major, built on a rolling upright-piano chord pattern played with the sustain half down and an M1-style organ bass doubling the root. 125 BPM, kick punchy and dry, shuffled sixteenth hats, claps a shade late so the groove grins from the hips, tambourine on the offbeat pushing from behind. Intro is piano and tambourine with the low end filtered out; the tension is eight bars of the same pattern moving up a whole step while the room reverb grows; the release drops the full kick and bass under those chords, weight coming off the chest. Breakdown strips to piano and one held organ note, then the last third stacks the chords an octave up and rides the hats hotter than is polite. Warehouse doors rolled open at six in the morning, light across the concrete, nobody leaving. Early-90s New Jersey garage rooms, Strictly Rhythm directness.

## p3 — Filter as light

French filter house in B-flat major, one looped disco string phrase and a played bass riff carrying the whole record. 127 BPM, kick fat and compressed, the mix pumping under sidechain like it is breathing, closed hats tight on the sixteenths. It starts almost dark with the low-pass down around 400 Hz and climbs for its whole length, the filter opening a little every eight bars, the strings gaining air until the release lands wide and bright all at once. Breakdown shuts the filter halfway and leaves the bass and a single clap alone in the room, then the last third opens further than it ever opened before and sets a horn stab in answer to the strings. A wet street in morning light, the last cab of the night pulling away. 1997 Paris filter house, Roulé-era discipline.

## p4 — Afro house, golden hour

Afro house in D mixolydian, carried by a dry log-drum sub landing just off the beat and an mbira figure that repeats until it stops sounding like a melody. 122 BPM, kick soft-edged and deep, shakers and hand drums layered thick enough to swing on their own, one rimshot a bar answering late. Sixteen bars of percussion alone, then the log drum arrives and does the work, and the first lift is a wide organ chord that holds under everything and stays put. Breakdown is the mbira and one sustained chord with a lot of air around them, then the last third brings the full percussion bed back with the organ up a fifth and the reverb longer. A rooftop at golden hour, concrete still holding the heat, drinks going warm. Cape Town afterhours patience, early Innervisions restraint.

## p5 — Sunrise trance

Euphoric progressive trance in C major, one rolling offbeat bass and a single glass-bright lead that climbs and never sits down. 138 BPM, kick short and tight, sixteenth hats rolling with an open hat on alternate upbeats, snare rolls saved for marking the eights. The build is long and honest, thirty-two bars of bass and hats under a string pad rising a semitone at a time, and the lead enters already at full brightness. Breakdown pulls the kick out entirely and leaves the pad and a delayed fragment of the lead hanging, then the last third puts the kick back under the lead an octave up and holds it there to the end. The last record of a day party, sun straight through the smoke, doors propped open. 1999 Gatecrasher dawn-set scale, sincerity played straight.
